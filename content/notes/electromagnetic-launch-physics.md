# What I Misunderstood About Electromagnetic Launch Systems

- **Date**: February 2026
- **Domain**: Aerospace Systems / Propulsion Physics / Modeling
- **Status**: Research Note

## Question
Can a ground-based electromagnetic track eliminate the first stage of an orbital space launch vehicle?

## Observation
My initial conceptual models assumed that achieving high muzzle exit velocity ($>1.5\text{ km/s}$) at sea level would directly translate to massive orbital payload mass fractions.
However, modeling atmospheric re-entry thermodynamics in reverse revealed severe aerodynamic heating and extreme dynamic pressure ($Q > 250\text{ kPa}$) in the lower troposphere. The structural mass required for thermal protection on the projectile would counteract much of the propellant mass savings.

## What Changed
- Re-scoped ESCL-II from a pure orbital launcher to an "upper-stage velocity assist" system.
- Re-evaluated the track exit profile to moderate velocity ($300\text{–}600\text{ m/s}$) with an elevated high-altitude incline exit, significantly reducing lower-troposphere aerodynamic stress.
- Focused the concept paper on structural g-load constraints on small satellite electronics (assessing survivability up to $25\text{ g}$) and ground power storage sizing using pulse-power capacitor banks.
